Mailmeteor vs OutMass for Outlook

Published June 12, 2026 · updated August 31, 2026 · 6 min read

Mailmeteor sells an Outlook add-in through Microsoft AppSource, alongside its Gmail, Sheets and Excel products. So if your mailbox is on Outlook or Microsoft 365, both it and OutMass are real options, and the choice is a real comparison rather than a platform question.

Both tools do personalized bulk email from an Outlook or Microsoft 365 mailbox, both send through your own account, and both track what happens afterwards. Below: where they genuinely differ, the two places OutMass is behind, and how to move across if you decide to.

Quick facts. Both work with Outlook. Mailmeteor is an AppSource add-in with its own web dashboard, and also has Gmail, Sheets and Excel products. OutMass is a Chrome and Edge extension that runs inside Outlook on the web, takes a CSV upload, and sends through your own Outlook account via Microsoft Graph. OutMass free tier: 250 emails/month, forever, no footer.

Two different ways into the same mailbox

The real difference is not the platform any more — it is where the tool lives.

  • Mailmeteor is an Office add-in installed from AppSource, with a web dashboard alongside it. Add-ins reach the desktop Outlook app and Mac as well as the web, which is genuinely wider reach than a browser extension has.
  • OutMass is a Chrome and Edge extension. It only works in Outlook on the web — outlook.office.com, outlook.live.com — and it puts the campaign panel inside that page rather than in a separate dashboard.

If you run Outlook in the desktop app and rarely open it in a browser, that difference decides it before any feature does.

The sending ceiling is Microsoft's, not ours. Whichever tool you use, sending from Outlook / Microsoft 365 is bounded by 10,000 recipients/day, up to 500 recipients per message and a 30-messages-per-minute throttle (Exchange Online limits). Personal Outlook.com accounts sit far lower, around 500 recipients/day, and brand-new Microsoft 365 tenants are throttled below the headline number for their first 30 days. Where OutMass is genuinely different

OneDrive attachment links instead of raw files

Attaching a file to a cold send is one of the fastest ways into the spam folder. OutMass leans the other way: when you add an attachment, it generates a view-only sharing link from your own OneDrive and drops it into the message instead of bolting a heavy file onto every copy. The recipient still gets the document, you keep deliverability closer to a plain-text email, and large files stop being a problem. Read the other way, this is a limitation: if you need a real per-recipient attachment — an invoice, a certificate — OutMass cannot do it and Mailmeteor can. Pick accordingly.

Reply detection, on every plan

Open rates are noisy — since iOS 15 (2021), Apple's Mail Privacy Protection pre-loads tracking pixels for Apple Mail users regardless of whether the message was actually read, which inflates reported opens, while Outlook's default image-blocking deflates them. Replies don't lie. OutMass runs a daily scan of your Inbox via Microsoft Graph and stamps any matching contact as "replied," then surfaces a reply rate alongside opens and clicks in the campaign report. It's usually a smaller number, but it's the one you can actually trust. Mailmeteor tracks replies too; the difference is that OutMass does not put it behind a paid tier — it is on the free plan, because a number you cannot see is not a number you can act on.

"Engaged" — one honest engagement number

OutMass's Engaged metric counts the distinct contacts who opened, clicked, OR replied. In a world where opens are unreliable and clicks miss the people who reply without clicking, it's the closest thing to a single trustworthy engagement signal, and we have not seen another Outlook merge tool roll it up into one number.

Resume after a partial send failure

If a campaign hits a transient network blip or a Microsoft Graph 5xx mid-send, OutMass marks it "partial" and shows a Resume button on the report detail view. One click retries only the still-pending recipients — no duplicate sends, no manual cleanup. It's a recovery flow built specifically for the way Graph sending behaves.

What you give up — and when Mailmeteor is the better choice

The honest section, and it is longer than it used to be. Now that both tools do Outlook, several things genuinely favour Mailmeteor:

  • You use the Outlook desktop app or Mac Outlook. This is the big one. An AppSource add-in loads there; a browser extension does not. OutMass only works in Outlook on the web, and if that is not where you read your mail, nothing else on this page matters.
  • You need multi-stage follow-up sequences. Mailmeteor chains follow-ups until someone replies. OutMass sends one follow-up stage. If you run 5–6 step cadences, that is a real gap.
  • You need real file attachments. Per-recipient invoices, payslips, certificates — OutMass sends OneDrive links, not files. If the file itself has to arrive, this rules OutMass out.
  • You want live spreadsheet editing. OutMass takes a CSV upload per campaign rather than a continuously-synced sheet.
  • Team features. Shared templates across users and role-based access are not in OutMass yet.

What is left on our side is narrower and more specific: reply detection and tracking that are not gated behind a paid tier, subject A/B testing, an Engaged number that combines opens, clicks and replies, resume after a partial send, and a panel that lives inside the Outlook tab you already have open rather than in a second dashboard. If you send from Outlook on the web and those are the things you care about, OutMass is the better fit. If any of the five above is a deal-breaker, it is not — and we would rather you found that out here than after installing.

How to move a campaign across

  1. Export your contact list to CSV. Whatever holds it today — a Mailmeteor sheet, Excel, your CRM. Columns named email, firstName and company are exactly what OutMass expects.
  2. Install OutMass from the Chrome Web Store and sign in with the Outlook account you actually want to send from.
  3. Recreate your templates in the Campaign tab (the Templates dropdown above the subject line). OutMass merge tags use double-brace syntax — {{firstName}}, {{company}} — so a quick find-and-replace on your existing copy will do it.
  4. Re-import the CSV into a new campaign, preview the merge, and you're ready to send.

Tracking history is per-platform — open and click data don't carry across tools because the tracking pixels and link redirects are domain-specific — so your reporting starts fresh on the OutMass side. It's cleaner to wind down an in-flight Mailmeteor sequence and restart it on OutMass than to migrate mid-stream.

OutMass.
