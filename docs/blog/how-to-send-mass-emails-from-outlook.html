How to Send Mass Emails from Outlook in 2026 (Step-by-Step)

Published April 29, 2026 · updated October 11, 2026 · 13 min read

Outlook is one of the most widely used email apps there is, but it has a reputation for being painful to use for mass email. The default app gives you no real way to send a personalized message to hundreds of recipients without copy-pasting, hitting send limits, or landing in spam.

This guide walks through every realistic option — from the built-in Word + Outlook mail merge to dedicated tools — so you can pick the right one for your situation. We'll cover daily send limits, deliverability, and what each method gives you (and quietly takes away).

Sending separately: If each person should get their own copy rather than one shared message, new Outlook and Outlook on the web have a built-in Start mail merge that does it with the same text for everyone. For that and the personalized options, see how to send a mass email individually in Outlook.

The short version: If you're sending fewer than 50 personalized emails a month and have plenty of patience, Word's mail merge wizard does the job in classic Outlook. For everything else — scheduling, tracking, follow-ups, more than 100 recipients — you'll save hours and get better deliverability with a purpose-built tool. OutMass is the one we make.

Method 1: Word + Outlook mail merge (free, native, painful)

In classic Outlook, Microsoft's answer to mass email is Word's mail merge feature. It works, but it's slower and more limited than most people expect.

Classic Outlook only. Word drives this merge through MAPI, which the new Outlook does not expose — if you have been moved to new Outlook, Word will try to open classic Outlook and the merge will not complete. What mail merge does and does not do in new Outlook →

How to do it

  1. Prepare your contact list as a Word document, an Excel file, or an Access database. The first row needs column headers like FirstName, Email, Company.
  2. Open Word → Mailings tab → Start Mail Merge → Email Messages.
  3. Click Select Recipients → Use an Existing List and choose your file.
  4. Write the message. Use Insert Merge Field to drop placeholders like «FirstName» wherever you want personalization.
  5. Finish & Merge → Send Email Messages. Choose the email column, enter a subject, and click OK.

Word will hand each personalized message to Outlook one by one. If you have 200 contacts, Outlook will spend a couple of minutes sending them in the background.

What you don't get

  • No HTML formatting beyond Word's basics. Custom buttons, branded headers, multi-column layouts — forget it.
  • No scheduling. The merge runs the moment you press the button, so you can't send at 9 a.m. in your recipient's timezone.
  • No tracking. You have no idea who opened, who clicked, or who replied — unless you manually inspect every reply.
  • No follow-ups. If a recipient doesn't reply, you'd have to set up a second merge from scratch.
  • No suppression list. If someone unsubscribes, you have to remember to remove them yourself.
  • The "From" field can't be a delegated mailbox. You can only send as your primary account.

For a one-time announcement to a small list, this is fine. For ongoing outbound, the missing pieces add up fast.

Method 2: Outlook distribution lists / contact groups

Distribution lists let you address one email to a group, but they're closer to a CC: than a real mail merge. Put a contact group in To or Cc and recipients can see every member's address; put your own address in To and the group in Bcc and the list is hidden, but everyone still gets the same message with no personalization. To send so each person sees only their own address, see how to send a mass email individually in Outlook.

When this works

  • Internal team announcements where everyone knows everyone.
  • Family or club newsletters where addresses being visible is fine.
  • Any case where you don't need {{firstName}}-style personalization.

When this falls apart

Distribution lists are not appropriate for cold outreach, sales sequences, or anything where the recipient should believe they got an individual message. They also count against the per-message recipient limit, which adds up To, Cc and Bcc and counts each member of a contact group separately; Microsoft lists it as customizable up to 1,000 recipients on a Microsoft 365 business mailbox (many mailboxes default to 500).

Method 3: VBA scripts

If you're comfortable with code, Outlook lets you write VBA macros that loop through a CSV or Excel sheet and call MailItem.Send for each row. There are dozens of tutorials online; the basic shape is fewer than 60 lines of code.

VBA gives you total control: custom HTML, attachments, conditional logic, scheduling via timers. You can also hook into Outlook events to track replies. Microsoft documents the object model in detail at learn.microsoft.com/office/vba/api/overview/outlook.

The catches

  • VBA runs only on Windows desktop Outlook. Outlook on the web, Outlook for Mac, and the new Outlook for Windows do not support it.
  • Modern security policies in Microsoft 365 often block macro-driven sends entirely or trigger anti-malware scans on every message.
  • You're now maintaining a script. Recipients change, columns change, and a tiny bug can email the wrong people the wrong message.
  • No tracking unless you build it yourself.

VBA is a viable answer for a single technical user solving a one-off problem on a Windows desktop. It's not a sustainable solution for sales or marketing teams.

Outlook's daily and per-minute send limits

Whichever method you use, you're sending through Microsoft's servers and have to live with their throttles. Microsoft publishes the official numbers at learn.microsoft.com; here is the practical version:

Account typePer dayPer minutePer message

Microsoft also says, on the same Exchange Online page, that its mailboxes aren't meant for bulk commercial email such as newsletters to large lists. Treat these numbers as a ceiling, not a target: an account that sends many similar emails to people who don't know it can be stopped well before it reaches them.

The new-tenant row is a different kind of limit from the others. Microsoft's tenant external recipient rate limit caps how many people outside your organization the whole tenant can email per day, based on its license count. A tenant less than 31 days old gets 10% of that standard quota, and 25% from day 31 to day 60.

If you exceed the per-minute throughput, Microsoft holds the extra messages and sends them over the following minutes. On a Microsoft 365 work or school mailbox, if you exceed the per-day limit, you get a "submission quota exceeded" non-delivery report (code 5.2.2) and can't send again until the number of recipients you messaged in the past 24 hours drops below the limit. It is a rolling window, not a reset at a fixed hour. On a personal Outlook.com account, Microsoft may instead pause sending and ask you to verify the account; follow the instructions in your Outlook inbox before you send again.

Deliverability: how to actually land in the inbox

The biggest mistake people make with mass email from Outlook is treating it like a marketing platform. Outlook is a personal mailbox. The send patterns that get past spam filters look like a person, not a machine.

  • Personalize the subject and the first line. "Hi {{firstName}}" in the body isn't enough — variation in the subject is what separates an email a human wrote from a bulk send.
  • Keep the message text-heavy. Long blocks of text, conversational tone, and short signatures look like real correspondence. Marketing-template HTML with images and gradient headers looks like marketing.
  • Skip attachments for cold outreach. A 1 MB PDF triggers more spam filters than a link to the same PDF on OneDrive.
  • Send during business hours in the recipient's timezone. A batch that lands at 3 a.m. looks like a machine, not a person.
  • Spread the send. Microsoft caps sending at 30 messages a minute, so leave at least 2 seconds between messages.
  • Always include a real unsubscribe link. Someone who can't opt out tends to press "Report spam" instead, and those reports count against you.
  • Put your postal address in the email. If you're sending commercial email to people in the US, CAN-SPAM requires it, along with a working opt-out.

Frequently asked questions

How many emails can I send from Outlook per day?

Microsoft publishes a daily figure for some Outlook.com accounts and not others. Microsoft 365 subscribers: 5,000 recipients a day, 500 per message, and 1,000 a day to people you have not emailed before. Free accounts: no figure is published — Microsoft says limits are lower and vary with account history. Microsoft 365 business and Exchange Online accounts allow up to 10,000 recipients per day, with a per-message limit of 500 by default (an admin can raise it to 1,000) and a per-minute throughput cap of 30 messages.

Can Outlook do mail merge natively?

Classic Outlook needs Microsoft Word for it: Word drives a mail merge that uses Outlook as the sender, but the workflow has serious limitations: no scheduling, no open tracking, no follow-ups, and no easy way to reuse the same data source across campaigns. New Outlook and Outlook on the web have a basic built-in mail merge that sends everyone their own copy of the same message, but it cannot personalize each email yet. Microsoft's personalized version (roadmap item 423047) was listed for September 2026; on 10 October 2026 it was still "In development".

Can I put each person's name in the subject line?

Not with Word's mail merge: you type one subject for everyone, and only the body takes fields from your list. In OutMass any column of your CSV works in the subject as well as the body, on every plan.

Can I add an attachment to a mail merge?

Word's mail merge from Windows can't attach a file to each email; the usual workaround is a link to the file. OutMass works the same way, built in: you pick files from your OneDrive and each email carries them as links (up to 10), shown as buttons under your message. Recipients see a link, not an attachment, which also keeps your emails light. The first time, Microsoft asks you to allow OneDrive access, and on a work or school account that often needs your IT administrator's approval.

Will my mass emails go to spam?

Mass emails from Outlook generally land in the inbox if you respect three rules: stay below the daily and per-minute limits, use a personalized subject and body for each recipient, and include a working unsubscribe link. Bulk-attachment campaigns are far more likely to be filtered than text or link-based ones.

Is mail merge legal?

Personalized email at scale is legal in most jurisdictions when you have a legitimate basis to contact the recipient, identify yourself clearly, and offer an unsubscribe option. CAN-SPAM (US), GDPR (EU), KVKK (TR) and similar laws all apply — make sure your sender identity, business address, and opt-out are real. OutMass adds the opt-out link to every email; your postal address you type into the message yourself.

OutMass runs in Outlook on the web (outlook.office.com, outlook.live.com and outlook.cloud.microsoft) in Chrome or Microsoft Edge, on Windows or Mac. It doesn't run inside the desktop apps: the new Outlook for Windows, classic Outlook or Outlook for Mac. If your account is Outlook.com, Hotmail or Microsoft 365, open Outlook on the web in your browser with it instead; it is the same mailbox. Gmail, Yahoo, iCloud and other accounts added to Outlook can't send through OutMass.
