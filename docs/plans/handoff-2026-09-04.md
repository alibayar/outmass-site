## The work, and why

### 1. A long-stalled campaign must not resume silently — BACKEND, DO FIRST

**Why:** this is faisal's bug, and his email says it is fixed. Nothing else
should ship before it.

**What:** a campaign that has sent nothing for longer than one billing cycle
does not resume on its own. It emails the owner and waits for the Resume
button, which already exists (`btnResumeCampaign`, 14 locales).

**Why one billing cycle and not less.** Four live places promise automatic
resumption after a quota reset:

| Where | Words |
|---|---|
| Store listing, 12 languages | "hit your monthly quota mid-campaign? The rest sends automatically after your reset" |
| `send_quota_capped_email` | "sends them automatically once the quota resets" |
| `alertQuotaCapped`, 14 locales | "They stay saved and will be sent…" |
| The email owed to hrds | "They send automatically when your allowance resets on 16 September" |

All four are about **quota**, and a quota hold is bounded by construction — the
reset is at most a month away. hrds waits 13 days. A threshold above one cycle
therefore breaks none of them.

What creates unbounded waits is the **dormancy** hold, which is what caught
faisal at 45 days. Separating the two is the whole fix.

**Honest caveat:** this narrows the claim for a user who is away for two
months. The listing does not promise "however long you are gone", but it does
not exclude it either. Ali accepted the narrowing; it is the right direction,
because a send two months later with no warning is exactly the incident.

**Not retroactive.** faisal is already archived; hrds is inside a cycle.

### 2. Pause a schedule that runs past 31 days — EXTENSION, 0.3.5

Ali's proposal, and it is a good one: at scheduling time, compute the end
date. If it runs past 31 days, everything beyond that pauses until the user
comes in and says continue, so they learn at setup that they need to check in.

**Why it is worth doing.** faisal's remaining draft is 1,210 recipients. At
5/day that is 242 days. Nothing today tells anyone that.

**Why it does not replace item 1.** Wave 6 had `daily_send_cap` NULL and no
`scheduled_for` — a send-now that stopped. There was no end date to compute,
so this rule would never have fired on the campaign that caused the incident.
Two different holes: a predictable long run, and an unpredictable long pause.

**Why it needs a store round.** The value is in telling the user *while they
are setting it up*. That is panel work.

### 3. A campaign shows what it says and not how it sends — BACKEND then PANEL

`GET /campaigns/{id}/stats` returns subject, body, counts and rates and **none
of** `scheduled_for`, `daily_send_cap`, `send_days`, `attachments`. The panel
reads `send_days` in exactly one place: the create payload.

**Why:** Hélène asked for a setting, we gave it to her by UPDATE, and she had
no way to see it. She rebuilt two campaigns from scratch. It is the same
defect 0.3.3 fixed for the message, and the comment shipped with that fix says
why: *"a person who cannot read their own campaign has to ask us instead."*

**Smallest version:** return the four fields, render them read-only. No editing.

### 4. Editing a running campaign's schedule — LATER, depends on 3

There is no way to change `send_days`, `daily_send_cap` or `scheduled_for`
after a campaign starts, which is why a routine request became an UPDATE
against a paying customer's live row. It went cleanly because §4.5 forced a
full read of the row first — the same discipline that found `{{hook}}` in her
body a fortnight ago. **The safety came from a habit, not the system.**

### 5. Payment intent arrives a day late — SMALL

`checkout_abandoned` rides Stripe's `checkout.session.expired`, which fires
~24h after an unpaid session. Ravi abandoned his at 09:01 on 09-03; the alert
landed 09:01 on 09-04, by which time we had already found, fixed, shipped and
emailed. `checkout_session_created` with no `subscription_created` after ~30
minutes is the same fact while the person is still at the desk.

**Read it right:** Ravi's was not a payment problem. He thought the
restriction he hit was a plan limit. An alert that sends someone to look at a
declined card would have missed it.

## What this session should have taught us

Five times the data corrected an assumption I had stated as fact, and every
one had the same shape: **reading a number without asking what produced it.**

- `max(timestamp)` said faisal was back. It was `ext_updated` — the browser
  updating the extension.
- A 19% sign-in rate looked like a collapsed funnel. It was one stuck user's
  23 attempts.
- `status = 'suppressed'` returned zero, which read as "nobody was held".
  Suppression lives in its own table.
- `config.py` defaults `FIRST_SIGNIN_INCLUDE_MAIL_READ` to true. Production
  had been false for five days.
- The privacy claim was fixed in `listings.json` and still live in the store.
  **The repo is not the world.**

Three of the five were caught by Ali asking an ordinary question — "did you
update the store?", "is this backend only?", "will pushing email hrds?". The
last one found a defect in a fix I had written twenty minutes earlier: the
notification would have fired every six hours for thirteen days, about fifty-
two emails.

That question is now a rule in the global CLAUDE.md, along with its sharper
half: for anything that sends a message, **"does it break?" is not enough —
ask "how often does it fire?"**
