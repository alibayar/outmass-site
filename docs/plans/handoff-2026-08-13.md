  One "Upgrade Plan" button called CREATE_CHECKOUT with a hardcoded `starter`;
  the price was invisible until the payment form and **Pro could not be bought

  emitted a PostHog event and a log line and nothing else, which is how five
  warm leads went unanswered. Telegram alert, gated on `if not
  already_subscribed` so a completed upgrade never fires it.

  `cancellation_requested` stays silent (Mary Bass would have received a

  `payment_failed` gets a message that positions itself as the end of a chain
  rather than news, a promo expiry never mentions a card. Unknown reason is
  treated as *not their choice* — the cost of guessing wrong is one email.
- **76d6a3d — the panel explains the cap to someone whose plan just ended.**
  `plan_ended_recently` = free, has a `stripe_customer_id`, dropped within
  `PLAN_DROP_NOTICE_DAYS`.

  Found while checking something else. `outlook.cloud.microsoft` has been in
  the manifest since 0.1.23 and is where a cancelling customer's last recorded

  conclude they were in the wrong place. The manifest is now the test's source

## The thread running through the whole day: absence does not announce itself

Every defect this session shares one property — **nothing failed loudly, and
nobody would have complained.**

- `sidebar_opened` ate funnel data for months. No error, no alert; the number
  was simply smaller than the truth.
- An abandoned checkout logged a line nobody reads. Five warm leads.
- A plan dropped to free and said nothing. The customer discovers it by
  hitting the cap mid-send, which is the worst possible moment.
- The welcome email listed the wrong hosts. The reader concludes *they* are in
  the wrong place, and leaves.
- `grep -v test` swallowed a line of real code and a feature shipped
  incomplete believing it was whole.

The operational consequence: **the checks worth running are the ones whose
failure mode is a shrug.** Today three such checks were run and two came back
clean — which is the point, not a waste. The third was a real defect and was
visible only because someone looked.

## Lessons this session earned

- **Check the thing whose failure would be silent, even when you expect it to
  pass.** Two of three came back clean today. The one that did not was found
  only because the check was run at all.
- **A number in customer copy rots.** `terms.html` promised a 50-email free
  tier for months after config.py said 250. Every quota assertion now patches
  the limit to a value the tier has never had, so a reinstated literal cannot
  coincide with the expected answer.
- **Distinguish what the customer chose from what happened to them.** The same
  event — plan drops to free — is noise in one case and essential in the
  other, and Stripe tells them apart in a field we were not reading.
- **A control that reads "show me options" must not behave like "pay now."**
  The old Upgrade button converted the already-convinced and lost everyone who
  wanted information first.
